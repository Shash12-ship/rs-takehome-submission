\begin{abstract}
I organize the autoresearch run around three results. First, one attention head is an
affine-over-positive-affine rational atom, so an $H$-head score is a sum of $H$ such
atoms. Second, clearing the positive denominators gives the useful lower bound
$\tdeg(f)\leq \Hstar(f)$, and this bound is exact for every symmetric Boolean
function. Third, it is not exact in general: the four-pair Hamming-distance threshold
$\mathbf 1[\Delta(x,y)\geq2]$ has threshold degree two but head complexity three.
These form a coherent spine: a model-native normal form, a classical relaxation, and
a natural example showing what that relaxation loses.

I then use the four-pair separation to obtain a result not stated in the run. For every
$m\geq4$ and $2\leq t\leq m-2$, the Hamming-distance threshold
$\HDTH_{m,t}$ has threshold degree two but needs at least three heads. The two endpoint
thresholds $t=1$ and $t=m$ have exact head complexity two. The proof is short: every
interior member contains the four-pair separation as a restriction. I also give a
five-level forbidden-window criterion for arbitrary Hamming-distance profiles and an
exact Python verifier for all finite identities and certificates used here.
\end{abstract}

\section{Notation and evidence policy}

Write $y_f(x)=2f(x)-1\in\{-1,+1\}$. A polynomial $p$ \emph{sign-represents}
$f$ if $y_f(x)p(x)>0$ on the Boolean cube, and $\tdeg(f)$ is the least degree of
such a polynomial. I use $\Hstar(f)$ for the minimum number of heads in the model from
the task statement. Constants have head complexity zero.

The theorem directory mixes conceptual results, finite classifications, and increasingly
specialized upper-bound invariants. I used two filters. A result should either change the
way I think about $\Hstar$, or open a lower-bound route that can separate it from familiar
complexity measures. I also distinguished a proof from numerical evidence. The exact
integer verifiers for the eight-bit separation and its three-head certificate both pass;
I do not treat failed optimization runs as lower bounds.

\section{Part One: the results I would keep}

\subsection{The rational-atom normal form is the structural core}

At the query token, the query vector of a head is independent of the input. If the bit at
position $i$ is $x_i$, its unnormalized attention weight has the form

\[
\rho_i\alpha^{x_i}, \qquad \rho_i>0,\quad \alpha>0.
\]

After composing the head's value and output maps with the final linear readout, its scalar
contribution is

\[
\phi(x)=
\frac{\eta+\sum_{i=1}^n\rho_i\alpha^{x_i}(m_i+\delta x_i)}
{\gamma+\sum_{i=1}^n\rho_i\alpha^{x_i}},
\qquad \gamma>0.
\]

Since $x_i$ is Boolean, $\alpha^{x_i}=1+(\alpha-1)x_i$. Both numerator and
denominator are therefore affine on the cube. The denominator is strictly positive, and
all of its nonzero slopes have the same sign. Conversely, if $A$ is affine and $B$ is a
strictly positive affine form whose slopes are all positive, all negative, or all zero,
the parameters above can be chosen so that $\phi=A/B$. Thus the model has the following
exact scalar form.

\begin{proposition}[linear-fractional normal form]
A Boolean function is computable with $H$ heads exactly when it is sign-represented by
\[
S(x)=c+\sum_{h=1}^{H}\frac{A_h(x)}{B_h(x)},
\]
where every $A_h,B_h$ is affine, $B_h>0$ on the cube, and every nonconstant $B_h$
has one common slope orientation.
\end{proposition}

The reverse direction is constructive. Give each desired atom its own orthogonal block in
the model space, use the token-embedding difference to create $\log\alpha$, use positional
embeddings to create $\log\rho_i$ and $m_i$, and let the readout sum the blocks. This is
why dimension is not a hidden restriction in $\Hstar$.

This proposition matters more than the many later compiler bounds. It removes irrelevant
parameterization details and exposes the actual unit of computation. It also explains a
real asymmetry: complementing all inputs preserves the atom class, but complementing an
arbitrary subset can turn a globally oriented denominator into a mixed-orientation one.
That point becomes important in Part Two.

As an immediate check, a single ratio $A/B$ has the same sign as $A$ because $B>0$.
Hence one head computes exactly the nonconstant linear threshold functions. This cleanly
settles the first nontrivial level of the hierarchy.

\subsection{Threshold degree is the right first relaxation}

Suppose $S=c+\sum_h A_h/B_h$ strictly sign-represents $f$. Multiplying by the
positive common denominator gives

\[
P(x)=c\prod_{h=1}^{H}B_h(x)
+\sum_{h=1}^{H}A_h(x)\prod_{g\neq h}B_g(x).
\]

This polynomial has degree at most $H$ and exactly the signs of $S$. Therefore

\begin{proposition}[threshold-degree lower bound]
For every Boolean function $f$,
\[
\tdeg(f)\leq \Hstar(f).
\]
\end{proposition}

This proof is short, general, and reusable. More importantly, it is tight on a whole
class rather than only on parity. Let $f(x)=F(|x|)$ be symmetric, and let $C(F)$ be
the number of label changes in the sequence $F(0),\ldots,F(n)$.

For the lower bound, any sign polynomial can be symmetrized without changing its degree
or signs. On a Hamming layer it becomes a univariate polynomial in $|x|$. Each label
change forces a distinct real root between consecutive integers, so its degree is at
least $C(F)$.

For the upper bound, choose a degree-$C(F)$ univariate polynomial $p$ whose roots lie
exactly between the changing layers. Pick distinct positive $r_1,\ldots,r_C$ and write

\[
\frac{p(z)}{\prod_{j=1}^{C}(z+r_j)}
=c+\sum_{j=1}^{C}\frac{d_j}{z+r_j}.
\]

Each reciprocal $d_j/(|x|+r_j)$ is one valid head. The denominator on the left is
positive at every Hamming weight, so the rational score retains the signs of $p$.
Consequently,

\begin{theorem}[symmetric functions]
If $f(x)=F(|x|)$, then
\[
\Hstar(f)=\tdeg(f)=C(F).
\]
\end{theorem}

This theorem is the clearest positive answer in the run. It gives a human-readable
meaning to one head: on a one-dimensional positive statistic, one head buys one sign
change. It also suggests a productive strategy for upper bounds: find a positive
projection that collapses the truth table, then count only its label changes.

\subsection{The four-pair separation shows what threshold degree forgets}

For $x,y\in\cube^4$, define

\[
\Delta(x,y)=\sum_{i=1}^{4}(x_i+y_i-2x_i y_i),\qquad
h(x,y)=\mathbf 1[\Delta(x,y)\geq2].
\]

The quadratic $\Delta-3/2$ sign-represents $h$. Fixing one pair unequal and two
pairs equal leaves XOR on the remaining pair, so $h$ is not an LTF. Hence
$\tdeg(h)=2$.

The important result is that the threshold-degree lower bound is strict:

\begin{theorem}[four-pair Hamming separation]
\[
\tdeg(h)=2<3=\Hstar(h).
\]
\end{theorem}

I summarize the lower-bound argument because it identifies the extra structure that a
two-head polynomial must retain. Clearing two heads gives

\[
P=(cB+A)D+CB,
\]

a sum of two products of affine forms, with $B,D$ positive and globally oriented. This is
far more constrained than an arbitrary quadratic sign polynomial.

First, write any quadratic sign-representing $h$ as
$p_X(x)+p_Y(y)+x^{\mathsf T}Ky$. Checkerboard rectangles between the distance-one
and distance-two shells force the symmetric part $(K+K^{\mathsf T})/2$ to be negative
definite. In particular, $K$ is nonsingular. Fourteen explicit rectangles suffice: after
simultaneous signed coordinate permutations, their direction inequalities control every
ordered vector $z_1\geq z_2\geq z_3\geq z_4\geq0$ by expanding it in prefix vectors.

Second, the factorization $P=AD+CB$ can be written as the pullback of
$q(z)=z_1z_2+z_3z_4$, a quadratic form of inertia $(2,2)$. An actual denominator
becomes a null covector for the dual form. Its positivity and common orientation impose
strict positive slope and intercept constraints.

Finally, transitions from the distance-one shell to the distance-two shell normalize
these constraints into a symmetrizable $4\times4$ matrix $M$. They force

\[
\operatorname{tr}(M)+2\sum_{j=1}^{4}\max_{i\neq j}M_{ij}<0.
\]

But a column-max spectral lemma says the same expression is positive whenever
$M\mathbf 1>0$ and $M$ has at least two positive eigenvalues. The factor quadratic
provides exactly those two positive directions, giving a contradiction. Thus two heads
are impossible. The matching three-head certificate is reproduced in Appendix~\ref{app:certificate}
and has exact minimum signed cleared margin $58$.

This is the result I would prioritize after the normal form. It uses a natural function,
not a truth table found only by search, and it proves that polynomial degree loses the
factor geometry created by clearing attention denominators.

\subsection{Research judgment on the rest of the run}

The three results above form a compact research story. I would next invest in the
slice-rank-two observation for cleared scores: for any $H\geq2$, the homogeneous cleared
numerator can be written $L_1Q_1+L_2Q_2$, with one $L_i$ an actual denominator. This
abstracts the two-head factorization and may yield reusable algebraic lower bounds.
At present it is a relaxation, not yet a practical certificate in general, so I would test
it first on the Hamming family below.

I would deprioritize introducing further named upper-cost invariants unless they close a
known gap or become efficiently optimizable. Many are correct compilers, but the main
uncertainty is now lower bounds beyond threshold degree. A short invariant hierarchy is
more useful than many incomparable sufficient conditions.

\section{Part Two: an infinite Hamming-threshold family}

For $x,y\in\cube^m$ and $1\leq t\leq m$, define

\[
\HDTH_{m,t}(x,y)=\mathbf 1[\Delta(x,y)\geq t].
\]

\begin{theorem}[family-level consequence]\label{thm:family}
Let $m\geq4$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item For every $1\leq t\leq m$, $\tdeg(\HDTH_{m,t})=2$.
\item The endpoint functions satisfy
\[
\Hstar(\HDTH_{m,1})=\Hstar(\HDTH_{m,m})=2.
\]
\item For every $2\leq t\leq m-2$,
\[
3\leq\Hstar(\HDTH_{m,t})\leq m+1.
\]
Thus every threshold in this range is a strict separation from threshold degree.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For (i), the polynomial
\[
p_{m,t}(x,y)=\Delta(x,y)-\left(t-\frac12\right)
\]
is a strict quadratic sign representation. For the reverse inequality, fix $t-1$ pairs
to be unequal, leave one pair $(a,b)$ free, and fix the remaining $m-t$ pairs to be
equal. The restricted predicate is $a\oplus b$. Since XOR has threshold degree two,
restriction monotonicity gives $\tdeg(\HDTH_{m,t})\geq2$.

For the first endpoint in (ii), $\HDTH_{m,1}$ is non-equality. Let
\[
X=\sum_{i=1}^{m}2^{i-1}x_i,\qquad
Y=\sum_{i=1}^{m}2^{i-1}y_i.
\]
The two-atom score
\[
S=\frac{4X}{1+X+Y}+\frac{1-5X-Y}{1+X+2Y}
\]
has cleared numerator $1-(X-Y)^2$. A small positive threshold makes it strictly
positive exactly when $X=Y$; negating the score computes non-equality. Both denominators
are positive affine forms with positive slopes, so the two ratios are valid heads.

For $t=m$, put $N=2^m-1$ and $Z=X+Y$. The condition that every pair differs is
equivalent to $Z=N$. The polynomial
\[
q(Z)=\frac14-(Z-N)^2
\]
is positive exactly at the integer level $Z=N$. Divide by the positive polynomial
$(Z+1)(Z+2)$ and use partial fractions:
\[
\frac{q(Z)}{(Z+1)(Z+2)}
=-1+\frac{-(N+\tfrac12)(N+\tfrac32)}{Z+1}
+\frac{(N+\tfrac32)(N+\tfrac52)}{Z+2}.
\]
Each reciprocal is one head because $Z$ is a positive weighted sum of the input bits.
This proves the two-head upper bound for the second endpoint. The XOR restriction from
(i) and the threshold-degree lower bound give two matching lower bounds.

For (iii), fix $t-2$ pairs to be unequal and $m-t-2$ pairs to be equal, leaving four
pairs free. These counts are nonnegative exactly when $2\leq t\leq m-2$. On this
restriction,
\[
\Delta_m=(t-2)+\Delta_4,
\]
and therefore
\[
\HDTH_{m,t}=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \Delta_4\geq2.
\]
The restricted function is precisely the four-pair separation $h$, whose head complexity
is three. Head complexity cannot increase when variables are fixed, so
$\Hstar(\HDTH_{m,t})\geq3$.

For the upper bound, $p_{m,t}$ has one affine part and the $m$ nonlinear monomials
$x_i y_i$. The affine-free sparse-polynomial compiler uses one head for the affine part
and one for each nonlinear monomial. Therefore
\[
\Hstar(\HDTH_{m,t})\leq m+1.
\]
\end{proof}

The restriction argument is not specific to thresholds.

\begin{corollary}[five-level forbidden window]\label{cor:window}
Let $g_F(x,y)=F(\Delta(x,y))$ on $m$ pairs. If for some $0\leq r\leq m-4$ the
five labels
\[
(F(r),F(r+1),F(r+2),F(r+3),F(r+4))
\]
are $00111$ or $11000$, then $\Hstar(g_F)\geq3$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Fix $r$ pairs to be unequal and all but four of the remaining pairs to be equal. The
restricted distance is $r+\Delta_4$. The first label pattern gives $h$; the second gives
its output complement. Output complementation preserves the number of heads.
\end{proof}

This corollary is a local obstruction in the profile sequence. It can be checked before
attempting any global rational optimization.

\subsection{Why this extension is useful}

The base separation is only one function on eight bits. Theorem~\ref{thm:family} shows
that its obstruction is stable under a natural operation: adding fixed local pairs around
a four-pair core. It therefore gives a family of explicit, growing-input examples rather
than a single exceptional truth table. The lower bound remains three, so this is not yet
an asymptotically growing gap, but it establishes that the gap survives at every larger
pair count and across many threshold locations.

The proof also gives a cheap screening rule. Before searching over rational atoms for a
Hamming-distance profile, scan its length-five windows. A window from
Corollary~\ref{cor:window} immediately rules out two heads. This separates the discrete
part of the problem, finding a hard restriction, from the continuous part, searching for
admissible denominators and numerators. That is the kind of reduction I would want an
autoresearch system to surface early.

\subsection{What remains open}

The theorem deliberately stops at $t=m-2$. The near-endpoint threshold $t=m-1$
restricts on four free pairs to the pattern $00011$, not to the certified pattern
$00111$. It is tempting to reverse Hamming distance by complementing one of the two
strings, but arbitrary coordinatewise input complementation is not known to preserve
$\Hstar$ in this model: it can break the common orientation of a head denominator.
Treating that transformation as a symmetry would therefore be an error.

The first concrete next question is $\Hstar(\HDTH_{4,3})$. A two-head certificate would
expose a striking endpoint asymmetry; a lower bound would extend Theorem~\ref{thm:family}
to every non-endpoint threshold. The second question is whether the lower bound three is
tight uniformly for $2\leq t\leq m-2$. Both are small, testable targets for the exact
oriented-denominator search developed in the run. If the base lower bound is later
formalized in Lean, the family theorem itself should require only general lemmas for
restriction monotonicity and the explicit coordinate restriction above.

\section{Verification and limitations}

The accompanying script \texttt{submission/verify\_hamming\_threshold\_family.py}
uses only integer arithmetic. It performs four checks:

\begin{enumerate}
\item the quadratic sign polynomial and XOR restriction for every tested $(m,t)$;
\item every four-pair restriction used in Theorem~\ref{thm:family};
\item explicit two-head endpoint certificates through eight pairs;
\item the archived three-head certificate for $\HDTH_{4,2}$ on all $256$ inputs.
\end{enumerate}

Seven standard-library unit tests cover the same components. The main family theorem is
analytic; finite enumeration checks its constructions and guards against indexing or
sign mistakes. I have not formally verified the new theorem in Lean. The substantive
remaining dependence is the analytic two-head lower bound for $\HDTH_{4,2}$ reviewed in
Part One.

\section{Exact three-head certificate for the base separation}\label{app:certificate}

Order the variables as
$z=(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4,y_1,y_2,y_3,y_4)$. Define

\begin{align*}
D_1&=34-z_1-6z_2-z_3-8z_4-z_5-z_6-z_7-z_8,\\
D_2&=31-4z_1-z_2-3z_3-7z_4-5z_5-6z_6-3z_7-z_8,\\
D_3&=32-z_1-z_2-6z_3-z_4-z_5-6z_6-6z_7-4z_8.
\end{align*}

Their ranges on the cube are respectively $[14,34]$, $[1,31]$, and $[6,32]$.
All slopes are negative and every denominator remains positive, so each has valid
orientation. The numerators are

\begin{align*}
A_1={}&-476+1794z_1+2403z_2+2934z_3+132z_4\\
&+1890z_5-4130z_6+2868z_7-661z_8,\\
A_2={}&622-2333z_1-1471z_2+188z_3+3074z_4\\
&-2633z_5+2202z_6+208z_7-1392z_8,\\
A_3={}&-1006+1501z_1-577z_2-1950z_3-4044z_4\\
&+1799z_5+1406z_6-1914z_7+2472z_8.
\end{align*}

The score
\[
1+\frac{A_1}{D_1}+\frac{A_2}{D_2}+\frac{A_3}{D_3}
\]
has the target signs. After multiplication by $D_1D_2D_3$, exact evaluation gives
minimum signed margin $58$ over the $256$ vertices.
